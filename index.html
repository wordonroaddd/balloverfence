Legend — how to read this board
The one rule
100 = average
Nearly every number here is an index where 100 is league average. Higher = better for hitters, lower = better for pitchers. Rough guide: 105+ is a good hitting spot, 110+ is great, under 95 leans pitcher-friendly.
Game cards (best hitting conditions ranked first)
Total score
The one number to rank games by. Mix of ballpark + weather (55%), how beatable the two pitching staffs are (30%), and how good the two offenses are (15%).
Run environment
How run-friendly this stadium plays today: the park’s history plus temperature and wind.
HR environment
Same idea, but for home runs. Reacts more to heat and wind — this is the number for HR bets.
park run / park HR
The stadium’s baseline from the last 3 seasons, before today’s weather is added. Park HR is split by batter handedness (LHB / RHB) where the park is asymmetric — Yankee Stadium’s short right porch is a 128 for lefty bats but only 108 for righties, Houston’s Crawford Boxes flip it the other way. The HR sim uses the factor for the side each hitter actually bats from against each pitcher (switch hitters flip).
🌡 / wind / precip
Forecast at first pitch. Wind out = blowing toward the fences, helps hitters. Wind in = toward home plate, helps pitchers. “Cross” ≈ neutral. “Roof closed” = weather ignored.
Pitchers
ERA · HR/9 · K/9
Runs allowed per 9 innings, homers allowed per 9, strikeouts per 9. High HR/9 + low K/9 = the guy you want your hitters facing.
attack / avoid
Our 0–100 “beatability” rating (50 = average pitcher). attack (58+) = target his opponents’ hitters. avoid (42 and under) = tough arm. “Small sample” = few innings this year, rating pulled toward 50.
pen:
Same stats for that team’s bullpen (relievers only), with its own 0–100 beatability badge. “% LHP IP” = share of relief innings thrown by lefties — matters for platoon hitters late in games.
Hitter targets (appear once lineups post)
B / vs
Which side the hitter bats from (L/R/S) and which hand the starter throws with.
◆
Platoon edge: lefty bat vs righty starter, righty bat vs lefty starter, or switch hitter.
OPS vs SP
The hitter’s actual season numbers against pitchers of the starter’s hand. (OPS: .700 ≈ average, .800 good, .900+ elite.)
OPS vs Pen
His expected numbers against that bullpen, blending his lefty/righty splits by how many innings the pen’s lefties vs righties throw.
Target
The one number to rank hitters by: today’s HR environment + how beatable the whole opposing staff is (assumes ~55% of at-bats vs the starter, ~45% vs the pen) + his matchup stats. 100 = average hitter in an average spot; 115+ is a strong play.
Model lines
Proj score / Fair total
The model’s projected final score and combined runs, from each offense’s quality vs the other side’s full staff, scaled by today’s park + weather. Compare fair total to the book’s over/under — lean over when the book is well under it.
Fair ML / −1.5
Break-even moneyline for each side, and the break-even price on the favorite winning by 2+ (the −1.5 run line). Same rule everywhere: the book paying more than fair = model value.
Home run simulator
HR prob
Chance the hitter homers at least once today, from 10,000 simulated games. Typical everyday hitter ≈ 8–12%; elite sluggers in great spots reach 25%+.
Fair odds
Calculated from the simulation, not pulled from any sportsbook. It’s the break-even price for the simulated probability (20% ≈ +400, 12% ≈ +733). Compare it to your book: if they offer longer odds than fair, the model sees value; shorter means no edge.
HR·xHR
Actual homers · Statcast expected homers, based on how hard and far he’s actually hitting the ball (park-neutral). The sim scales each hitter’s rate by xHR-vs-HR: an unlucky crusher (xHR higher, ↑) gets boosted, a lucky wall-scraper (↓) gets discounted. Capped so it can’t more than roughly ±40% a rate.
Slot / *
Lineup position (higher slots bat more often). * = lineup not posted yet, so the sim used the team’s most-used players in projected order.
L / R / S
Batting stance, shown next to the hitter’s name. Switch hitters show S with their stronger side bolded (e.g. LS = better numbers batting lefty, by split OPS).
▸ and +
The two small buttons starting every sim row: ▸ expands a breakdown of exactly why that hitter’s number is what it is (split matchup, park factor for his batting side, weather, Statcast luck, bullpen, per-PA math). + adds him to My ticket in the tracker (✓ = already on it; click again to remove). A 🤖 next to a hitter’s name marks a leg on Pal’s suggested ticket.
LIVE rows
That game is in progress — the chance covers only the rest of the game (fewer at-bats left, and bullpen arms more likely), so live numbers run lower than pre-game ones.
Report card
Once games finish, the model grades itself: every homer actually hit, what chance the model gave that hitter before the game, and whether homer-hitters rated above the day’s average. One day is noise — judge it over weeks.
HR parlay tile
The sim’s top pick from each of the N best games (one per game, so multiplying their chances is roughly valid), with the combined chance and its fair price. Set the legs. Caution: books pay parlays far below fair — this shows what the ticket is worth, and most long parlays are entertainment, not value.
Ticket tracker
Two tickets, both graded live: 🤖 Pal’s pick — the sim’s auto-built suggestion, locked for the day (survives reloads); when upcoming games run out Pal fills legs from in-progress games at rest-of-game odds, so he always has action until the slate ends — and 🧑 My ticket, yours to build with the + on any sim row (✕ removes a leg). 💾 Save files your ticket as its own card below the tracker and clears the builder for the next one. Legs turn HIT ✓ the moment that hitter homers; tickets read CASHED or BUSTED. Adding a hitter who already homered today makes it an “another HR” leg — shown as “(2nd HR)” — which only cashes on a new homer, priced at his rest-of-game odds. Set legs to 1 for a straight HR bet. Calculators accept American (+12000) or decimal (12.5, DK Pick6-style) odds.

Full explanations are in the Legend above.

Data: MLB Stats API (schedule, probables, lineups, player stats, venue coordinates/orientation/elevation), Open-Meteo (hourly forecast), and Baseball Savant (Statcast expected home runs). Park factors are embedded editable constants (Statcast 3-yr style, 100 = neutral).

Recreational use only. This is a descriptive environment model, not a projection system or betting advice. It knows nothing about odds, injuries, bullpens, or lineups beyond what’s posted. Please gamble responsibly.
